Water damage cleanup in Seaford Harbor

Seaford Harbor is the waterfront community in the southern part of Seaford, NY. Ocean Avenue, Riverside Drive and Bayview Street are among its streets, and Seaford Harbor School sits at 3500 Bayview Street. We describe the area by those landmarks, because no source we found gives official boundaries.

This page is for homeowners there who have water inside the house. The cause matters, because canal or bay water, a backed-up storm drain and a split supply line are cleaned and insured in different ways. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, and no page on this site claims that every street here floods.

What local sources say about water in Seaford Harbor

The Seaford Harbor Civic Association has studied the neighborhood's anchorage lots, small public waterfront parcels that developer O.L. Schwenke set aside in the 1920s so buyers could reach the canals. A 2013 Long Island Herald report notes that one historian counted 15 to 20 of them, and that some are fenced or in poor repair.

During Superstorm Sandy in October 2012, an Alcona Street resident told the Herald in 2014 that water reached the bottom of his front door and collapsed his garage doors. He also said that once the water rises to a certain level it backs up through the storm drains. That is one resident's account, not a count of damaged homes. The National Weather Service's Freeport impacts sheet separately lists canal overflows in Seaford during Hurricane Irene in August 2011.

Nassau County's hazard mitigation plan lists Seaford among communities with mapped coastal high-hazard zones, and rates flooding as highly likely in any year across the county. That is a community-level statement, not a finding about a single street.

First response and safety

Water near a home's electrical system is the first hazard, not the cleanup. Do not wade into flooded rooms when outlets, appliances or a panel may be wet.

  • Keep people and pets away from water that may be contaminated or touching electrics.
  • Do not operate breakers or plug in equipment while standing in water.
  • Shut off the main water valve only if it is safe and accessible.
  • Photograph the damage and the waterline when it is safe to do so.
  • Move dry belongings to higher ground only where you can do so without entering the water.
  • Call 911 or your utility for gas odor, downed lines or any immediate danger.

How extraction and drying work after water like this

Crews remove standing water first, then dry the structure. Floodwater and sewage need contaminated materials assessed before drying starts, and household fans or an ordinary vacuum are not a safe substitute. Drying follows a plan with moisture readings, air movers and dehumidifiers, described on the structural drying page. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard that guides this work, and you can ask any company how it applies it. Not every wet material can be saved.

Insurance documentation after water damage

The New York Department of Financial Services says a typical homeowners or renters policy does not cover flood. Coverage for backup or other causes may come from an endorsement or from a separate flood policy. What applies to you depends on the cause of the water and your policy.

Photos taken before cleanup, notes on when the water arrived and moisture readings can help describe the loss. Call your insurer promptly, and ask them what your policy covers. Nothing here promises coverage or claim approval.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Seaford Harbor?

The dispatch line is answered 24/7, including nights and weekends. Call after you have checked that the area is safe from electrical hazards. No arrival time is promised, and availability is confirmed on the call.

Is canal or bay water different from a plumbing leak?

Yes. Water from outside is generally treated as Category 3, grossly contaminated, so affected materials are handled more carefully than after a clean supply-line leak.

Does homeowners insurance cover canal flooding?

Usually not under a standard policy, according to the New York Department of Financial Services. Ask your insurer about a flood policy or a water back-up endorsement.

Should I turn the power off if water is in the house?

Do not touch the panel or any switch while standing in water. If you cannot reach the panel safely, call your electric utility or 911 and stay out of the wet area.

The water has already drained away. Is cleanup still needed?

Often yes. Wet drywall, insulation and subfloors can stay damp after the water you can see is gone, and moisture readings show whether drying is still needed.
